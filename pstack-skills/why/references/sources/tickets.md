# Issues and tickets

## What this source contains

Feature and defect reports, product or business requirements, parent initiatives and subtasks, project specs, discussion comments, labels, milestones, scope and status changes, attachments, duplicates, and linked code reviews.

## How to search deeply

Start with identifiers linked from commits and reviews. Read the complete issue and comments, then search related issues by feature name, symbols, error text, and business language. Follow parent initiatives: a subtask may explain what to implement while its parent explains why. Read attached or linked project documents. Check milestones, labels, dates, and scope changes. Follow duplicate chains to the canonical record.

## What strong evidence looks like

A specific motivating problem, customer or compliance requirement, explicit choice between alternatives, deadline or initiative that forced a tradeoff, or a linked spec connecting the requirement to the target. Labels suggest a motivation category but do not replace a stated reason.

## Common pitfalls

Closed or reopened tickets may have changed scope. Templates can contain empty boilerplate rather than rationale. Old plans may differ from what shipped. Duplicate records may omit the original discussion. Restricted issues or missing history are gaps, not permission to guess. Compare the issue chronology and final scope with the code and review dates.
