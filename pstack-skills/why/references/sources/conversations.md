# Conversations

## What this source contains

Design deliberation, incident discussions, review coordination, explanations by authors and maintainers, customer or product context, post-merge reconsideration, and decisions that never reached a formal document. Only conversations actually accessible through the available tools count as searched.

## How to search deeply

Search by author around the change or deployment dates, feature and symbol names, exact error strings, and review URLs or identifiers. Try informal phrasing and spelling variations. Narrow to relevant project, engineering, design, or incident channels. Read whole threads and surrounding context; an isolated message can mean something different in its replies. Follow linked reviews, tickets and meeting notes.

## What strong evidence looks like

An explicit tradeoff discussion, considered answer from an author or maintainer, incident explanation tied to the code, or a specific product/customer requirement linked to the change. Preserve quotations with attribution, thread locator and dates.

## Common pitfalls

Retention, deleted messages, archived channels, private conversations and unsearchable DMs can hide the record. Jokes, casual suggestions, and a message preceding a commit are not automatically decisions. Partial threads lose context. Authentication failure is an access gap: stop that source search and report it rather than inventing findings.
