# Long-form documents

## What this source contains

PRDs, technical specs, RFCs, ADRs, alternatives considered, design-review and meeting notes, domain context, strategy, runbooks, and incident postmortems. Sources include local repository documents as well as accessible document stores.

## How to search deeply

Search feature names, symbols, user-facing terms, authors, errors, and relevant dates. Read full documents rather than previews. Follow linked pages, appendices, meeting notes, drafts and revisions where available. Search beyond linked documents when the paper trail is thin; rationale may live in an unlinked note or an author's project space. Identify whether a document was a proposal, accepted decision, or later description.

## What strong evidence looks like

A specific problem statement, accepted ADR, alternatives and reasons for rejecting them, a postmortem action item connected to the target, or meeting notes recording a decision with matching author and chronology.

## Common pitfalls

A plan is not proof that it was implemented. Documents drift or remain stale; compare with shipped code and reviews. Multiple drafts or later rewrites can obscure the original decision. Boilerplate motivation sections may say little. Missing links, inaccessible pages, or unavailable revision history limit confidence; do not select a newer, tidier narrative merely because it is easier to find.
