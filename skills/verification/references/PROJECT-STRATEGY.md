# Manage Project Verification Strategy

Use this workflow when deciding whether to add, change, or remove a durable verification decision that applies across work.
The absence of a separate strategy artifact does not trigger this workflow for routine work.

## 1. Admit project strategy

Determine whether the project has durable, cross-work verification decisions that are not sufficiently clear from a more authoritative source.
Apply the same test to every statement in an existing strategy.
Keep a separate strategy absent when applicable instructions, executable configuration, current documentation, and approved work-specific plans already provide sufficient guidance.
Do not use strategy to repeat this skill, list commands, restate configuration, describe product behavior, preserve work history, or record unapproved proposals.

This step is complete when every proposed or retained strategy statement closes a durable cross-work knowledge gap that another authority does not close sufficiently.

## 2. Establish the current facts

Inspect applicable instructions, accepted decisions, supported gates, executable configuration, verification tools, representative checks, and known instability relevant to the proposed strategy.
Treat historical tests as evidence, not accepted policy.
Research an unfamiliar mechanism when it could change the strategy.
Obtain approval from the applicable authority before running a consequential experiment.

This step is complete when current executable facts, accepted decisions, and unresolved proposals are distinguished.

## 3. Approve and record the strategy

Present the complete proposed strategy change to the applicable authority.
Obtain one approval before adding, changing, or removing a project-level verification decision.
If approval is declined, leave the accepted strategy unchanged and report the unresolved choice.

After approval, record only accepted current strategy that changes verification decisions across work.
Use the project's established strategy artifact when one exists.
Otherwise, use `VERIFICATION.md` when repository-local Markdown is an appropriate authoritative medium.
Use the artifact's established structure.
When no structure exists, organize only the accepted content needed by the project.
Strategy can describe recurring Material Risks, non-obvious evidence constraints, supported mechanism limits, mandatory gates, intentionally retained end-to-end evidence and the Risks it addresses, and accepted runtime or stability constraints.
Omit categories without accepted content.
Keep proposed mechanisms in the applicable work item or experiment until approval.
Remove a replaced statement in the same change.
If no project-specific strategy remains, remove the artifact after approval.

Strategy management is complete when either no separate strategy is justified or every retained statement is accepted, current, and consistent with relevant executable mechanisms and applicable instructions.
